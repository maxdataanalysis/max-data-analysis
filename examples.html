Counting and measuring cells across a large image batch

The problem: hundreds of fluorescence images per experiment, scored by hand, with counts that shift depending on who did them and when.

The tool: a local application that applies identical segmentation settings to every image in a folder, outlines each object, and exports one results table per experiment, plus the outlined images so you can check the work.

Applying the same gating strategy to every sample

The problem: gating re-drawn by hand per sample, so population percentages aren't strictly comparable between batches or between people.

The tool: a scripted pipeline that applies your agreed gating strategy to every file, outputs population statistics in one table, and flags samples that fall outside expected ranges for a human to review.

Plate assays · Illustrative, drag the handle to compare

From raw plate-reader exports to normalised results

The problem: a spreadsheet that grew over years, with manual copy-paste between plates, controls applied inconsistently, and no record of what was corrected.

The tool: a local application that reads your instrument's export format directly, applies controls and normalisation, fits standard or dose–response curves, flags failed wells, and writes out both the results and a log of every step applied.

Turning tracked positions into behavioural metrics

The problem: tracking software produces coordinates, but the metrics you actually report, distance, time in zone, entries, speed profiles, still get calculated by hand in a spreadsheet per animal.

The tool: a tool that ingests tracking output, computes your defined metrics per subject and per zone, and produces a summary table across the whole cohort in one run.

Detecting events across long multi-channel recordings

The problem: scrolling through long traces to mark events by eye, slow, tiring, and hard to defend as consistent between recordings or between people.

The tool: a detector using criteria you define once, applied identically across every channel and every recording, with detected events marked on the trace so you can verify them rather than trust them blindly.

Cleaning and combining messy field records

The problem: years of records across multiple spreadsheets, collected by different people with different conventions, inconsistent naming and gaps, hours of cleaning before any analysis can start.

The tool: a tool that applies your validation rules, standardises names and units, merges everything into one clean dataset, and reports exactly what it changed and what it couldn't resolve.
